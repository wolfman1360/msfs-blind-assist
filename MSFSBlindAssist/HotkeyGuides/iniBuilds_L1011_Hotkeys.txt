READ THIS FIRST - WHAT THIS VERSION COVERS

Choose iniBuilds L-1011 TriStar from the Aircraft menu. This version covers the TriStar's
switch panels: the flight engineer's station, the overhead, the main instrument panel and the
center console, in the order the TriStar's own preflight flows call for them, the glareshield
autopilot, the three INS units and two PMS units (Shift+M), and the Paper Flight Bag tablet
(Shift+T for the captain's, Ctrl+Shift+T for the first officer's).


THE PANELS

Sections (Ctrl+1): Engineer Station, Overhead, Main Panel, Center Console. The panels (Ctrl+2)
follow the preflight flows: the Engineer Station starts with Circuit Breakers, lights, passenger
oxygen and Electrical; the Overhead with the INS mode selectors and the engine fire handles; the
Center Console with INS 1, INS 2, INS 3, Captain PMS and First Officer PMS.

A switch is a combo box with the aircraft's own position words. A push button is a button. A
dimmer or temperature knob is a slider. A knob that only turns, such as a pressurization rate
knob, is a pair of buttons, "increase" and "decrease". A guard comes just before the switch it
covers.

When you change a control, MSFS Blind Assist moves it exactly the way a click in the cockpit
would. Nothing is spoken back - your screen reader already says what you chose - unless the
control could not be moved, when you hear why: "Battery unavailable" (the simulator connection
cannot carry the change), or "position unknown, try again in a moment" (a few switches must be
read before they may be moved; this clears within a second or two of connecting).

Each panel's gauges and warning lights are in its Status Display (Ctrl+3): fuel per tank,
hydraulic pressures, cabin altitude, engine EPR, N1, N2, temperatures and fuel flow, the radios,
and the lights on that panel.


TYPED VALUES - type, then press Set or Enter

  Altimeters (Main Panel section: Captain, First Officer and Standby Instruments) - type inches
  (29.92) or hectopascals (1013). You hear "Captain altimeter 1013, 29.92".
  NAV 1 and NAV 2 (Center Console, Navigation Radios) - megahertz, 108.00 to 117.95.
  COM 1, 2 and 3, active and standby (Center Console, VHF Radios) - megahertz, 118.000 to
  136.990. Setting an active frequency puts it in standby and swaps it in.
  Squawk (Center Console, Transponder) - four digits, each 0 to 7.

  From input mode, Ctrl+B sets all three altimeters from one entry, and Ctrl+N sets NAV 1 and
  NAV 2 with their courses (see INPUT MODE below).


THE AUTOPILOT

The glareshield autopilot is the first panel of the Main Panel section, "Autopilot". It starts in
the order of the manual's main panel preparation: both HSI navigation source switches, the
selected speed, the selected heading and the Heading button, both flight directors, the
Autopilot A and B engage switches (Command, CWS, Off) and an Autopilot disconnect button, both
courses, the selected altitude and its reference, and the captain's takeoff go-around (TOGA)
button. Then the rest of the glareshield from left to right: autothrottle, thrust management,
VNAV, vertical speed with its selected value, altitude hold, IAS hold, Mach hold, turbulence,
ILS, localizer, VOR, INS and back course, and last the captain's and first officer's autopilot
alert reset buttons. Each value has a text box right before its knob. The panel's Status Display
shows the pitch mode, the armed and captured modes, and the values the windows are set to.

A heading, navigation or pitch mode button only works while the autopilot or a flight director
is on; the TriStar turns it straight back off otherwise. So with both flight directors and the
autopilot off you hear "turn on a flight director or the autopilot first" and nothing is pressed.
Turning vertical speed mode on sets its target to the aircraft's current vertical speed, so turn
the mode on before you type a vertical speed; typed while the mode is off you hear "turn vertical
speed mode on first". Thrust management needs an autopilot in command and the PMS takeoff page
filled in; without them the TriStar turns it straight back off.

Ctrl+S, Ctrl+H, Ctrl+A and Ctrl+V (input mode) open a box for the speed, heading, altitude or
vertical speed, with the buttons that go with it:
  Speed: Autothrottle, Thrust management, IAS hold, Mach hold.
  Heading: Heading, INS.
  Altitude: Altitude hold, VNAV.
  Vertical speed: Vertical speed.
Each button shows its state. Press one and about a second later you hear what the aircraft did:
"Heading on", or "Thrust management off" when the aircraft did not take it. Type a value and
press Enter - speed 100 to 350 knots, altitude 0 to 50000 feet, vertical speed -6000 to 6000
feet per minute (both in steps of 100), heading 0 to 359 - and you hear the value set
("Heading 090"). The box stays open for another value; Escape closes it. Opening another box, or
Ctrl+B, closes the one that is open.

Ctrl+P (input mode) opens the autopilot window. Its first list reads the glareshield's windows
line by line as the cockpit draws them ("Speed: IAS 250", "Pitch: VS -1500", "Heading: 090"),
then the engaged modes, the armed and captured modes, the autothrottle, thrust management and
both autopilots. It updates while the window is open without moving your place. Below the list:
Flight director A and B, Turbulence, ILS, Localizer, VOR and Back course, each showing the
position the aircraft reports; then three one-shot buttons with no state: Autopilot disconnect,
the captain's autothrottle disconnect and the takeoff go-around button; then the Autopilot A and
B combo boxes (Command, CWS, Off). Nothing is spoken when you press a button: a switch's label
changes once the aircraft has moved it, and a disconnect is heard as "Autopilot A off" when the
aircraft moves the engage switch. Escape closes the window.


THE INS AND PMS WINDOW - Press left bracket, then Shift+M

The three INS units and both PMS units read as text, live, one unit at a time: choose INS 1, INS 2,
INS 3, Captain PMS or First officer PMS in the Unit box. Each INS and PMS panel on the Center
Console also has a button that opens the window on its unit. The window opens on the display: the
unit's state, the overhead mode selector (off, standby, align, nav or attitude, so "All INS NAV"
can be checked here), the display selector, the windows as words ("Present position: N 36 05.1,
W 115 08.0", "Leg: 0 to 1") and its lights; on a PMS the page title, then lines 1 to 6 for the line
keys, then its mode lights. When a PMS page changes you hear its title and the cursor goes to
line 1; when an INS moves to its next leg you hear "Leg 1 to 2".

What you type is read back once it has landed: "Entry, left window: 36051N", "1: GMT, entering:
1200Z", or "Entry closed" when the entry closes. A PMS value the unit refuses shows ERROR for about
a second and is read at once; an INS Insert the unit refuses changes nothing, so nothing is said.
An entry holding a character the unit has no key for is not sent at all, and stays in the box to
edit. The automatic waypoint loading button's label shows the state the INS reports ("Automatic
waypoint loading: on" or "off"), and your screen reader reads it when it changes.

The keys you type go to the unit in one go, so a whole present position is in well under a second,
and a selector you move in the window waits for keys that are still being sent. The window's own
call-outs - "Leg 1 to 2", a PMS's new page title and "Entry closed" - each have a row in Ctrl+M
("INS window leg changes", "INS window PMS page changes", "INS window entry closed"), all on until
you untick them. What you type is always read back, and so is a value the unit refuses.

  Enter (input box)  Send the keys you typed: digits, and on an INS the letters N, S, E, W, L
                     and R (the INS has no letter keys: N is 2, S is 8, E is 6, W is 4, L is 7,
                     R is 9). A dot is left out, so "36 05.1" types 36051. On a PMS a space is
                     the SP key; for a minus sign on the SAT line type 9 first.
  Ctrl+Enter         Insert
  Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+6   PMS line keys 1 to 6 (or F1 to F6, with the alternate layout in FMC Settings)
  Page Up / Down     PMS previous and next page; Right Arrow on the display, or Alt+Right, goes to
                     the next page
  Backspace          CLR on a PMS, CLEAR on an INS (on the display). On a PMS, Clear deletes the
                     last character and the entry stays open (Page Up or Page Down abandons it);
                     on an INS, Clear closes a position or waypoint entry and does nothing on the
                     cross track page.
  Alt+S              Go to the input box
  Alt+Home           Back to the display
  Escape             Close the window
  INS buttons        Insert, Clear, Hold, Track change; the display selector, waypoint selector
                     and auto/manual/remote selector; Enter present position (types the
                     simulator's position on the position page); automatic waypoint loading; the
                     waypoint list.
  PMS buttons        Insert, Clear, Next page, Previous page, Performance management, Line 1 key
                     to Line 6 key.

To enter the present position by hand: Display selector to Position, type N3605.1, Enter,
Ctrl+Enter, then W11508.0, Enter, Ctrl+Enter. To start navigating from waypoint 0 to 1: Track
change, type 01, Enter, Ctrl+Enter. To key a parallel offset: on the Cross track page type 1, then
L or R, then the miles with a tenth (1R50 is right 5.0 miles), Enter, then Ctrl+Enter. On the PMS
takeoff page: Ctrl+1, type the time (1200), Enter, Ctrl+Enter. For a position update select
Position first, then Hold (the manual's order, Hold then Position, does nothing in this aircraft).
Set all three auto/manual/remote selectors to Remote before typing waypoints, so they reach every
unit; in Remote the leg line is the pair being ranged, not a flown leg.


THE PAPER FLIGHT BAG TABLET - Press left bracket, then Shift+T (captain) or Ctrl+Shift+T (first officer)

Each pilot's tablet opens in its own window, read as a list of controls and text, live, in the
order the tablet shows them. The first eight items are its tabs: Menu, Ground, Load sheet, OFP,
Takeoff performance, Landing performance, Charts and Enroute map; the one shown says "current
page", and the window says the page's name when it changes. F5 reads the page again, F6 says
whether the tablet is connected, and Alt+F4 closes the window.

  Ground                Doors, ground power, air conditioning cart, jetway, ramp truck, luggage,
                        catering and pushback, each button saying its state ("Door 1 left:
                        closed"); after a press it reads its new state. Chocks and maintenance
                        opens the chocks, covers, cowl doors and technicians list.
  Load sheet            Fuel per tank, cargo and passengers, in thousands of pounds or kilograms
                        as the tablet shows. Type a value and press Enter: it is loaded the way
                        the tablet's own LOAD key loads it. SimBrief import loads the figures
                        of your SimBrief plan; Load settings chooses instant, timed or realistic
                        loading.
  OFP                   Reload OFP fetches your SimBrief plan (type your SimBrief ID first, in
                        Menu, Third party, Simbrief Id); the plan is then readable line by line.
                        Send to INS loads its en-route waypoints into INS 1 from the captain's
                        tablet, or INS 2 from the first officer's, nine at a time; the INS and PMS
                        window (Shift+M) shows them in that unit's waypoint list.
  Takeoff performance   Weight, pressure altitude and temperature (Sync from aircraft fills them),
                        takeoff flaps, Calculate, then the results: V1, VR, V2, the flap
                        retraction speeds and the EPR settings. Set bugs and EPR sets them.
  Landing performance   Weight, field elevation, runway condition and anti-skid, Calculate, the
                        approach speeds, Set bugs.
  Charts, Enroute map   Pictures, which cannot be read here; the Charts page offers the METAR
                        lookup.
  Menu                  Autocomplete (the flight engineer's flows: open a flow to hear each step
                        and whether it is set; a step's button completes it), Panel states,
                        Helper (top of descent calculator and timer, in-flight fuel manager),
                        Aircraft settings, Throttle calibration, Third party (SimBrief ID,
                        Navigraph code) and Audio.

While the window is open, the tablet's progress and result lines (a panel state's progress, the
descent calculator's result, the loading timer, the METAR answer) are read as they change, and its
alerts (the top of descent pause, a load or performance error) are spoken at once.


CIRCUIT BREAKERS

The Engineer Station's Circuit Breakers panel has an "Open circuit breaker list" button. The
window lists all 981 breakers, each with its number ("HYD IND QTY, breaker 4: in"); the ones
iniBuilds left unnamed read "Breaker 12: in". Type a name or a breaker number in Search to
narrow the list, tick "Show pulled breakers only" to see what is pulled, and press Space or
Enter on a breaker to pull it or push it in. The row changes to the new state. The states are
read when the window opens; Refresh reads them again.

BACKGROUND ANNOUNCEMENTS - Ctrl+M

Warning and caution lights are announced when they come on and when they go out ("Engine 1 fire
light on"), once they have stayed that way for about a second, so a flashing light is heard
once. The annunciator light test, which lights every lamp at once, is silent.

The autopilot is announced when a switch moves other than by your own press in MSFS Blind
Assist: an engage switch ("Autopilot A command", "Autopilot B off" - which is also how a
disconnect is heard), a flight director, the autothrottle or thrust management turning on or off
("Autothrottle off"), a mode button turning on or off ("Heading on", "Altitude hold off"), and
the armed and captured modes as they come on ("Localizer armed", "Localizer captured", "VOR
captured", "Glideslope armed", "Glideslope captured", "Altitude armed", "Flare", "Rollout").
Your own press is not spoken back while the switch sits where you put it; if the aircraft moves
it anywhere else - turning thrust management straight back off, say - you hear that.

The flap handle, the parking brake and the ground spoiler arm are announced when something
other than MSFS Blind Assist moves them - a hardware lever, or the aircraft's own automation
("Flaps 14 degrees", "Parking brake set"). Changes you make in MSFS Blind Assist are not.

The INS and PMS are announced on their own: each INS's ready to navigate, warning and battery
lights and INS 1's waypoint alert light (the units light it together while they fly the same
legs, and INS 1 feeds the captain's HSI and the autopilot; INS 3 when the instrument source
switch selects it); "INS 1 navigating" when a unit starts navigating by itself because its mode
selector was already at NAV while it aligned (its ready to navigate light flashes for about a
second then, too short to be spoken); and "PMS climb", "PMS cruise" and "PMS descent" when the
aircraft changes the PMS mode. While the INS and PMS window is open it also says a new INS leg, a
PMS's new page and "Entry closed", and each of those has its own row too.

Ctrl+M opens the monitor manager: type in the search box to narrow the list, use the Show filter
to see only the muted rows, and untick an item to stop it announcing. Your choices are
remembered between sessions.
